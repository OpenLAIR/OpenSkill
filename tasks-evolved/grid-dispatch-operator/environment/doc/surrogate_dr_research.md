### 1. Dataset-Level Statistical Invariants
These invariants must hold true for any valid solution output by the optimization engine. You can hard-code these as `assert` statements in your test.

*   **Power Balance (Nodal):** For every bus $i$, the sum of generation ($P_{g,i}$) minus the sum of load ($L_i$) must equal the net power flow out of the bus through connected branches ($P_{flow,ij}$):
    $$\sum_{g \in G_i} P_{g,i} - L_i = \sum_{j \in \text{adj}(i)} B_{ij}(\theta_i - \theta_j)$$
    *Assertion:* `assert abs(sum(gen_at_bus) - load_at_bus - net_flow_out) < 1e-4`
*   **Generator Capacity Coupling:** For each generator $i$, the sum of active power dispatch ($P_i$) and spinning reserve allocation ($R_i$) must not exceed the maximum capacity ($P_{max,i}$):
    $$P_i + R_i \le P_{max,i}$$
    *Assertion:* `assert (output_MW + reserve_MW) <= pmax_MW + 1e-6`
*   **Transmission Limits:** The absolute power flow on any branch $k$ must not exceed its rated capacity ($RateA_k$):
    $$|P_{flow,k}| \le RateA_k$$
    *Assertion:* `assert loading_pct <= 100.0 + 1e-6`
*   **Reserve Requirement:** The total system reserve must meet or exceed the required reserve ($R_{total} \ge R_{req}$):
    *Assertion:* `assert totals["reserve_MW"] >= required_reserve_MW - 1e-6`

### 2. Cross-Validation Approaches
To independently verify the output without relying on the primary solver:

*   **Linear Programming (LP) Duality Check:** Since DC-OPF is a convex optimization problem, the dual variables (shadow prices) at the optimum provide a powerful verification tool. If you use `cvxpy` to solve the problem, you can extract the dual variables (`constraints.dual_value`) for the power balance and capacity constraints.
    *   *Verification:* The shadow price of the power balance constraint at a bus is the Locational Marginal Price (LMP). You can verify that the LMPs are consistent with the marginal costs of the dispatched generators.
*   **Alternative Solver:** Use a different solver backend (e.g., if the primary uses `OSQP`, use `ECOS` or `SCIPY.LINPROG`) to solve the same `network.json` and compare the objective function value (Total Cost). The difference should be within a small tolerance (e.g., $10^{-3}$).

import pytest

def test_dispatch_feasibility():
